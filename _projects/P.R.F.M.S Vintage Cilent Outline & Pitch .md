# Suction For Removal: Spotted Lantern Fly Mitigation

**Team:** P.R.F.M.S. Vintage  
**Client(s):** Cornell CALS Extension | E&J Gallo Winery | National Grape  

---

##  Problem Statement
Grape growers are harvesting grapes for wine and juice production in upstate NY, but the presence of spotted lantern flies (SLFs) in the harvest introduces bitter, toxic quassinoids into the end product. 

Current methods of mechanical harvesting cannot effectively separate SLFs from the grapes. High precision is required for removal, as **as few as 1-2 SLFs per kilogram of grapes** is enough to warrant rejection of a 22-ton load.

##  Impact
Successfully mitigating this issue will yield the following results:
* **Increased Revenue & Decreased Waste:** If less than 2 SLFs per 1000 grams of fruit are present in the collection bin, the clients will increase the amount of grapes sold.
* **Preservation of Mechanical Harvesting:** If our solution can be effectively adapted to mechanical harvesting, growers will not need to resort to manual hand harvesting and can maintain their current harvesting pace.

---

##  Proposed Direction

### Concept A: Conveyor Belt Vacuum
**What it is:** An overhead vacuum attached above the conveyor belt after mechanical harvesting, but before the crop drops into the collection bin.

**How it would be used:** * A conveyor belt runs under a vacuum, which provides just enough suction to pull in SLFs.
* It will be positioned *after* the fans on the conveyor belt, ensuring it only interacts with grapes, SLFs, and minimal extra material (MOG).

**Why it’s better than the status quo:** * Adds a dedicated, secondary opportunity for pest removal.
* Targets SLFs when they are most vulnerable (dislodged and not attached to vines).

###  End-of-Semester Proof-of-Concept
By the end of the semester, we will:
1. Design and 3D print a nozzle to adapt to a commercial vacuum that can remove SLFs without affecting the grapes.
2. Machine a mount to attach the vacuum to the end of the conveyor belt.

### Key Risks & Unknowns

| Risk | Description | Testing & Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Risk 1: Suction Precision** | The vacuum needs precise suction and distancing to pick up an object weighing 0.5-1g (SLF) but leave objects weighing $\ge$ 5g (grapes). | Test the prototype vacuum using objects of varying masses at a controlled, fixed distance. |
| **Risk 2: Suction Consistency** | The vacuum may not pick up every targeted item and might have weaker suction zones underneath the nozzle. | Pass multiple 0.5-1g objects under the vacuum across different zones and observe/record the success rate. |

---

## Questions for the Client

1. **How likely are dislodged SLFs from the harvester to cling on to grapes?**
   * > **Decision affected:** If they cling tightly, we will need to implement a pre-vacuuming solution that physically dislodges the SLFs from the grapes.
2. **How easy is it to implement a bulky solution (like a vacuum) on the conveyor belt that connects the harvester and the collection bin?**
   * > **Decision affected:** If the current infrastructure cannot easily support this, we will have to modify the conveyor belt system itself.
3. **How much material other than grapes (MOG) remains after the fan stage?**
   * > **Decision affected:** If there is a high volume of debris, we will have to alter the suction power to remove it, as SLFs may cling onto this extra material instead of passing through cleanly.
